1// axiam Reactor — CONTRACT.md §22, the protocol core over a caller-supplied
2// transport.
3//
4// WHAT THIS SHIPS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT.
5//
6// §22.1–§22.8 and §22.14 in full: the §8 v2 verification set on the event, the
7// canonical serialization and MAC in both directions, the §22.5 registry and its
8// mutable-field allow-lists, §22.8's strictest-wins default, and the declarative
9// binder. What it does NOT do is open a connection. §22.11 defers only the
10// transport, because there is no maintained AMQP client for the targets this SDK
11// serves that this project is willing to vendor.
12//
13// That split is deliberate and it is the newer one. Until contract 1.28 this SDK
14// shipped nothing from §22 at all while the section still bound an integrator to
15// §22.1–§22.8 — so the half deferred for want of a *dependency* was the
16// transport, and the half left to hand-roll from prose was the **protocol**: v2
17// HMAC over a canonical serialization with a `null` signature placeholder,
18// freshness in both directions, nonce and correlation binding, the allow-lists.
19// That is the half with the sharp edges, none of them AMQP-shaped, and asking
20// every integrator to reimplement it is how a signing bug ships.
21//
22// THE TRANSPORT SEAM HAS EXACTLY TWO CAPABILITIES (§22.11 rule 1): take the next
23// delivery, and publish a reply to a named destination. It is not wider than
24// that on purpose — an interface that also exposed declare, bind or queue-name
25// derivation would hand an integrator the tools §22.1 forbids using.

46// constants, not on a comment. A reactor round trip is milliseconds; the check
